01

THE BOY FROM ROSARIO

ROSARIO, ARGENTINA

Before the world knew his name, before the number 10 became a symbol, before millions of people stood up just to watch him touch a football, there was only a little boy in Rosario.

His name was Lionel Andrés Messi.

He was born on June 24, 1987, in Rosario, Argentina.

There was nothing that day to tell the world that something extraordinary had just begun.

No stadium was waiting for him. No crowd was chanting his name. No one knew that one day, an entire generation would grow up watching the way he played football.

He was simply a child.

A quiet boy growing up in a football-loving city, surrounded by a family that loved him.

And then there was football.

It was already everywhere around him.

His brothers played. His family loved the game. The streets of Rosario breathed football.

And the little boy watched.

Then, slowly, he began to play.

A football at his feet seemed to change something inside him.

He was smaller than many of the other children. But when the ball came to him, something seemed to disappear.

The noise disappeared. The fear disappeared. Even the people around him seemed to disappear.

There was only Lionel... and the ball.

His grandmother, Celia, played a special role in those early years.

She believed in the little boy.

While others might have simply seen a child playing football, she saw something worth protecting.

She took him to Grandoli, the small local club where his football journey truly began.

He was still only a boy.

But even then, there was something different about the way he moved.

The ball seemed to stay close to him.

As if it already knew where he wanted to go.

But dreams do not always arrive without a price.

As Lionel grew older, his family learned that he had a growth hormone deficiency.

It was something no child should have to worry about.

Yet Lionel kept playing.

He kept running after the ball.

He kept dreaming.

And perhaps that was the beginning of the difference between the boy he was and the player he would become.

He did not know what the future would ask from him.

He only knew that football made him happy.

Then came the moment when a dream became a difficult choice.

The treatment he needed was expensive. His family did what they could. But eventually, the road ahead began to point somewhere far away.

Away from Rosario.

Away from the streets he knew.

Away from the place where a little boy had first learned to love the game.

Barcelona was waiting.

But Lionel did not know what Barcelona would become.

He did not know that one day he would walk onto the biggest stages in football.

He did not know that children around the world would wear his name on their backs.

He did not know that one day, millions would call him the greatest of his generation.

He was still just a boy.

A boy leaving the city that had raised him.

And perhaps that is what makes the beginning of his story so beautiful.

Before the trophies, there was sacrifice.

Before the fame, there was uncertainty.

Before the number 10, there was a little boy chasing a football.

And before the world called him Messi...

Rosario simply called him Lionel.

The boy from Rosario.

The world had not met Lionel Messi yet.

But his journey had already begun.

02

THE JOURNEY TO BARCELONA

BARCELONA, SPAIN — 2000

Rosario was home.

It was where Lionel had grown up. Where football had first become part of his life. Where his family, friends, and childhood memories lived.

But sometimes, a dream asks you to leave the place where it was born.

In 2000, Lionel Messi was only thirteen years old.

He was still a child.

But the road ahead was already becoming bigger than the streets of Rosario.

His family made a decision.

They would cross the Atlantic and take Lionel to Spain.

Barcelona was waiting.

But nobody knew what would happen next.

Lionel arrived in Barcelona in September 2000.

He came for a trial.

Not as a famous footballer. Not as a future legend.

Just as a thirteen-year-old boy carrying a dream.

And at first, even his size made people wonder.

He was small. He was thin.

To some, he did not look like the kind of player who could survive at the highest level.

But then the ball reached his feet.

And everything changed.

For two weeks, Lionel trained with Barcelona.

The people watching him began to see something that was difficult to explain.

His body was small.

But his football was not.

He moved with the ball as if it belonged to him.

He could run past players, change direction, and keep the ball close in a way that made people stop and watch.

Still, Barcelona did not immediately say yes.

There were doubts.

Some people were not convinced.

The future of a thirteen-year-old boy was impossible to guarantee.

His family returned to Argentina.

And then came the waiting.

Days passed.

Weeks passed.

But there was still no final answer.

For Lionel's family, this was not simply about football anymore.

Their son was far from home, and the future was uncertain.

They needed to know.

Would Barcelona take him?

Or would the family have to search for another path?

Then, in December 2000, everything changed.

Carles Rexach, Barcelona's technical director, watched Lionel play.

He did not need much time.

He had seen enough.

But there was one small problem.

There was no proper paper available for the agreement at that moment.

So Rexach took a napkin.

And on that small piece of paper, he wrote a promise.

Barcelona would sign Lionel Messi.

The date was 14 December 2000.

It looked like almost nothing.

Just a napkin.

A few handwritten words.

But nobody in that room could have known what those words would eventually mean.

That little piece of paper would become one of the most famous objects in football history.

But even after the agreement, the journey was not suddenly easy.

Lionel still had to leave everything familiar behind.

His hometown. His friends. His childhood.

And most importantly, his family.

Barcelona was now becoming his new world.

La Masia.

A place where young footballers were trained not only as players, but also as young people.

And Lionel entered that world as a quiet and shy thirteen-year-old.

He was far away from the boy who had grown up in Rosario.

Everything was new.

The language. The people. The football environment. The life.

And there was another obstacle.

Because of delays with his transfer, Lionel could not play official youth matches for around six months.

He could train.

He could play some friendlies.

But he had to wait.

Imagine being thirteen years old, travelling across an ocean for your dream, finally reaching the academy you hoped for— and then being told to wait.

Six months.

Six months of uncertainty.

Six months of watching, training, learning, and waiting for the moment when he could finally play.

But Lionel stayed.

He kept training.

He kept learning.

And slowly, the boy from Rosario began to find a place in Barcelona.

He was not yet a star.

He was not yet famous.

Nobody knew how far his story would go.

But the first door had opened.

Rosario had given him the beginning.

Barcelona had given him a chance.

Now, it was time for Lionel to prove what lived inside him.

The world still did not know his name.

But inside La Masia, a small boy was beginning to write a story that football would never forget.

He had crossed an ocean for a dream.

Now he had to discover what that dream would cost him.

And somewhere inside La Masia, the next chapter was waiting.

03

THE FIRST TOUCH

LA MASIA, BARCELONA — 2001–2004

Barcelona had opened the door.

But walking through that door was only the beginning.

Lionel was still thirteen.

He was far from the streets of Rosario, far from the football fields of his childhood, and now surrounded by players who were also dreaming of becoming professionals.

At La Masia, nobody was going to give him a place simply because Barcelona had chosen him.

He had to earn it.

And slowly, the little boy from Rosario began to show what he could do.

On 7 April 2001, Lionel Messi played his first official match for a Barcelona youth team.

He wore the number 9.

Barcelona faced Amposta.

They won 3–0.

And the thirteen-year-old scored.

It was only one goal.

One small moment in a youth football match.

But for Lionel, it was the beginning of something real.

He was no longer simply the boy who had arrived from Argentina.

He was beginning to belong.

Then, almost immediately, football reminded him that nothing would come easily.

In his second official match, against Tortosa, he suffered a fracture to his fibula.

Suddenly, the football stopped.

The boy who had travelled across an ocean to chase his dream had to watch from the side.

He had to wait again.

But waiting was becoming part of his story.

He waited before Barcelona accepted him.

He waited while his future was uncertain.

And now, he had to wait for his body to heal.

But he came back.

And when he returned, he kept moving forward.

U14.

U16.

U19.

One level after another.

He was growing older, but something else was happening too.

He was growing faster than the system expected.

His coaches kept moving him upward.

The competition became harder.

The players became stronger.

The game became faster.

But Lionel kept finding a way to make the ball feel simple.

He was small.

But he was difficult to stop.

The ball stayed close to his feet.

His movements were quick.

And when he saw a space, he seemed to reach it before everyone else.

Slowly, people inside Barcelona began to understand.

This was not simply another talented boy.

Something different was happening.

Then came 2003.

Lionel was sixteen years old.

Barcelona travelled to Portugal to play a friendly match against Porto.

Among the first-team players was a teenager from La Masia.

Lionel Messi.

In the 71st minute, he entered the pitch.

For the first time, he was wearing the Barcelona first-team shirt.

He was sixteen.

The stadium was not celebrating him.

The world was not watching him.

There were no headlines calling him a legend.

There was simply a young boy stepping onto a football pitch with a dream that had suddenly become much closer.

But the real debut was still waiting.

In March 2004, Messi made his debut for Barça B.

He was moving closer.

Youth football was behind him.

Senior football was beginning to appear in front of him.

And then came the night that changed everything.

16 October 2004.

Espanyol against Barcelona.

The match was already underway.

Barcelona were leading.

Then, in the 82nd minute, Frank Rijkaard made a substitution.

Deco came off.

A seventeen-year-old boy stepped onto the pitch.

His shirt carried the number 30.

His name was Lionel Messi.

It lasted only a few minutes.

He did not score.

He did not change the result.

He did not lift a trophy.

But something much bigger had happened.

The boy who had once left Rosario had finally reached Barcelona's first team.

Thirteen when he arrived.

Seventeen when he made his official debut.

Four years.

Four years of waiting, injuries, training, growing, learning, and believing.

And now, the door was no longer closed.

It was open.

The world still did not know what was coming.

Barcelona had only seen the first touch.

They had not yet seen the magic.

The boy from Rosario had finally arrived.

But this was not the beginning of his legend.

It was only the first glimpse of it.

04

THE LITTLE MAGICIAN

BARCELONA — 2005–2008

The boy had finally entered the first team.

But entering Barcelona was one thing.

Staying there was another.

Lionel Messi was still only seventeen.

Around him were players the football world already knew.

Ronaldinho. Eto'o. Deco. Xavi.

They were stars.

Messi was still the kid wearing number 30.

But the kid was beginning to make people look twice.

He was small.

He was quiet.

But when the ball came to his feet, he seemed to become someone else.

On 1 May 2005, that feeling became something real.

Barcelona were playing Albacete at Camp Nou.

Messi came onto the pitch near the end of the match.

And then, Ronaldinho found him.

Messi put the ball in the net.

But the goal was ruled out for offside.

The moment disappeared.

But there was still time for another.

Ronaldinho sent the ball forward again.

Messi moved toward it.

One touch.

Then another.

And this time, the ball went over the goalkeeper and into the net.

Goal.

Lionel Messi had scored his first official goal for Barcelona.

He was seventeen years old.

And standing beside him was the player who had begun to believe in him.

Ronaldinho.

The world had not yet given Messi a name.

But Barcelona was beginning to understand what it had.

The following seasons changed everything.

Messi became more important.

More confident.

More dangerous.

In 2006, Barcelona became European champions.

Messi was part of the team that lifted the Champions League trophy.

He was still young.

But he was no longer simply a boy waiting for his chance.

He was becoming part of Barcelona's future.

Then came 2007.

And one night changed the way people looked at him.

10 March.

Camp Nou.

Barcelona against Real Madrid.

El Clásico.

The biggest rivalry in Spanish football.

Real Madrid scored.

Barcelona answered.

Madrid scored again.

Barcelona came back again.

The game kept changing.

And then Messi took over.

He scored.

Then again.

And again.

Three goals.

A hat-trick against Real Madrid.

Messi was only nineteen.

Yet on one of football's biggest nights, the youngest player on the pitch had become the centre of everything.

The little boy was becoming a magician.

But he was not finished.

Just over a month later, another moment arrived.

18 April 2007.

Barcelona against Getafe.

Messi received the ball deep inside his own half.

Then he ran.

One defender disappeared.

Then another.

Then another.

He kept going.

The ball stayed close to his feet.

The defenders could not catch him.

The goalkeeper came forward.

Messi moved around him.

And finally, he placed the ball into the empty net.

Five opponents beaten.

One extraordinary run.

One goal that reminded the world of another famous run from another Argentine magician.

Diego Maradona.

Comparisons began.

People started asking how far this young player could go.

But Messi was still Messi.

He did not seem interested in becoming someone else.

He simply kept playing.

Then came 2008.

Ronaldinho left Barcelona.

The player who had once welcomed the young Messi was leaving the club.

But before leaving, something important was passed on.

The number 10.

The shirt Ronaldinho had worn was now Messi's.

It was more than a number.

It felt like a torch being passed from one magician to another.

Ronaldinho had helped light the way.

Now Messi had to walk it alone.

He was no longer the little boy from La Masia.

He was no longer simply Barcelona's young talent.

He was becoming something much bigger.

The number 10 was waiting.

And with it, an entire era.

First, they called him a boy.

Then they called him a magician.

Soon, the world would call him Number 10.

05

THE RISE OF NUMBER 10

BARCELONA — 2008–2012

Ronaldinho was gone.

The magician who had once welcomed the young boy from La Masia had left Barcelona.

And something was left behind.

The number 10.

Now, it belonged to Lionel Messi.

In the summer of 2008, Messi began wearing the famous shirt.

But nobody could have known what that number would eventually become.

It was only a number.

At least, that was what it looked like.

Then came another change.

Pep Guardiola became Barcelona's first-team coach.

A new coach.

A new number.

And the beginning of a new era.

Guardiola trusted Messi.

And slowly, Barcelona began to build around the little Argentine.

Xavi.

Iniesta.

Busquets.

And Messi.

The ball moved.

The team moved.

And somehow, Messi always seemed to be at the centre of it.

Then came the 2008–09 season.

Barcelona won La Liga.

They won the Copa del Rey.

And then came the Champions League.

The final was against Manchester United.

One of the biggest teams in Europe.

One of the biggest nights in football.

Barcelona won.

And Messi scored.

The boy who had once arrived in Barcelona as a thirteen-year-old was now scoring in a Champions League final.

But that was only the beginning.

2009 became something extraordinary.

Barcelona won six major trophies in the same calendar year.

Six.

La Liga.

Copa del Rey.

Champions League.

Spanish Super Cup.

European Super Cup.

Club World Cup.

And at the centre of it all was the number 10.

Messi was no longer Barcelona's young talent.

He was becoming the face of the team.

Then came another moment.

The Ballon d'Or.

In 2009, Lionel Messi won it for the first time.

He was twenty-two years old.

The world had finally caught up with what Barcelona had been watching for years.

The little boy was now officially one of the greatest players in the world.

But Messi did not stop.

He became even more dangerous.

Faster.

More confident.

More complete.

Around him, Xavi and Iniesta controlled the rhythm of the game.

And Messi found the spaces nobody else could see.

In 2010, something almost impossible happened.

Messi, Xavi, and Iniesta finished as the three finalists for the Ballon d'Or.

Three players.

One club.

All connected to the same football philosophy.

La Masia.

Then came 2011.

Wembley.

Another Champions League final.

Barcelona against Manchester United.

The same opponent.

Another enormous night.

Barcelona won 3–1.

And Messi scored.

The number 10 was now standing at the centre of one of the greatest teams football had ever seen.

Xavi.

Iniesta.

Busquets.

Piqué.

Dani Alves.

And in front of them, Messi.

The boy from Rosario had become the player Barcelona depended on.

But there was still something changing.

The number 10 was becoming more than a shirt number.

It was becoming an identity.

When people saw number 10, they began to think of Messi.

When they saw Messi, they thought of number 10.

The two had become almost impossible to separate.

And as the goals kept coming, the records began to fall.

The boy who once needed someone to believe in him was now making the world believe in him.

By 2012, Messi had reached a place few footballers had ever reached.

He was no longer chasing the great players of the past.

He was beginning to create his own history.

And somewhere beneath all the trophies, all the goals, and all the records, there was still the same boy.

The boy who had left Rosario.

The boy who had crossed an ocean for a dream.

The boy who once wore number 30.

Now, the whole world knew the number 10.

And they knew the name written above it.

Lionel Messi.

But football was about to give him another story.

Because greatness is rarely measured alone.

Soon, another name would stand beside his.

Another superstar.

Another number.

And an entire generation would begin to choose sides.

The boy had become Number 10.

But now, the world was ready for a rivalry.

Messi was about to meet his greatest contemporary challenger.

06

THE RIVALRY

BARCELONA & MADRID — 2009–2018

By now, the world knew Lionel Messi.

The number 10.

Barcelona's magician.

The boy from Rosario had become one of the biggest names in football.

But in 2009, another story was beginning.

Cristiano Ronaldo arrived at Real Madrid.

Suddenly, the greatest rivalry in football had a new stage.

Barcelona.

Real Madrid.

Messi.

Ronaldo.

Two players.

Two clubs.

Two completely different styles.

And one generation watching every moment.

Messi played the game as if the ball was connected to his feet.

Close control.

Dribbling.

Passing.

Vision.

Ronaldo was different.

Power.

Speed.

Movement.

A hunger for goals that seemed impossible to satisfy.

They were different.

But both wanted the same thing.

To be the best.

And every season, the question returned.

Who was better?

The answer never stayed simple.

One would score.

The other would answer.

One would break a record.

The other would chase it.

And the world kept watching.

But the rivalry was never only about two players.

It was Barcelona against Real Madrid.

El Clásico.

The biggest stage for the two of them.

Every time Messi walked onto the pitch against Madrid, millions watched.

Every time Ronaldo faced Barcelona, millions watched again.

In 2010, Barcelona produced one of the most memorable Clásico victories.

Camp Nou.

Barcelona 5.

Real Madrid 0.

But Madrid would not disappear.

They came back.

And in 2011, the rivalry reached another level.

Barcelona and Real Madrid met again and again.

La Liga.

Copa del Rey.

Champions League.

The matches became bigger.

The pressure became heavier.

And every goal felt like a message.

In the Champions League semi-final, Messi produced another unforgettable moment.

He received the ball.

He ran.

One defender.

Then another.

And suddenly, he was through.

Goal.

Barcelona moved toward another Champions League final.

But Madrid had their moments too.

In the Copa del Rey final, Cristiano Ronaldo scored the winning goal.

Nobody was running away.

The rivalry continued.

Season after season.

Goal after goal.

Record after record.

The Ballon d'Or became another battlefield.

Messi won it.

Ronaldo answered.

Messi won again.

Ronaldo answered again.

The competition never seemed to end.

And perhaps that was what made it so extraordinary.

They were not simply competing against the players around them.

They were constantly pushing each other higher.

The standards kept rising.

The goals kept coming.

The records kept falling.

And an entire generation grew up watching them.

Some chose Messi.

Some chose Ronaldo.

Some changed their minds every week.

But everyone watched.

Then came 2017.

Santiago Bernabéu.

Real Madrid against Barcelona.

The match was almost over.

And then Messi scored.

A late winner.

He stood in front of the Madrid crowd and celebrated with his shirt held high.

Number 10.

His name.

His moment.

Another page in the rivalry.

But every era eventually changes.

In 2018, Cristiano Ronaldo left Real Madrid.

After nine years, the rivalry inside Spain was coming to an end.

He left behind goals, trophies, records, and countless memories.

Messi stayed.

Barcelona stayed.

But something was different.

The football world had lost a rivalry it might never see again.

Two players.

Two different paths.

Two different ways of becoming great.

And for almost a decade, they had shared the same stage.

Maybe the real question was never simply who was better.

Maybe the real gift was that we got to see both of them at the same time.

Messi.

Ronaldo.

Two names that became part of the same era.

But while Messi was conquering Europe with Barcelona, there was another part of his story that still remained unfinished.

Argentina.

The country where his story had begun.

The country that had given him his first football.

The country where the boy from Rosario had first dreamed.

And there, the story would become much more painful.

For years, the world watched them compete.

But Messi's greatest battle was still waiting for him.

Not against Ronaldo.

Not against Barcelona's rivals.

But against the weight of an entire nation.

07

THE PAIN OF ARGENTINA

ARGENTINA — 2014–2016

With Barcelona, Lionel Messi had almost everything.

Trophies.

Records.

Champions League nights.

Ballon d'Ors.

And the number 10 had become a symbol of greatness.

But there was one thing he still could not hold.

A major trophy with Argentina.

And somehow, the country where his story had begun became the place where his heart would hurt the most.

Then came 2014.

Brazil.

The World Cup.

Messi was Argentina's captain.

And this time, the dream felt close.

Argentina kept moving forward.

Match after match.

Goal after goal.

Until they reached the semi-final.

Netherlands.

Ninety minutes.

No winner.

Extra time.

Still no winner.

Penalties.

Argentina survived.

And suddenly, the final was waiting.

13 July 2014.

Maracanã.

Argentina.

Germany.

One match between Messi and the dream.

The game remained without a goal.

Ninety minutes.

Then extra time.

And finally, Germany scored.

1–0.

The final whistle came.

Argentina had lost.

Messi had reached the biggest stage in international football.

But the trophy was not his.

He received the Golden Ball as the tournament's best player.

But that award could not replace the World Cup.

He had come so close.

And still, he went home without it.

Then came 2015.

Copa América.

Another tournament.

Another opportunity.

Argentina reached the final again.

Chile stood between Messi and the trophy.

Ninety minutes passed.

No goal.

Extra time came.

Still nothing.

Penalties.

And Argentina lost.

Again.

Another final.

Another empty-handed journey home.

But Messi stayed.

He tried again.

And then came 2016.

Copa América Centenario.

Argentina reached the final once more.

And once again, Chile was waiting.

The match stayed goalless.

Ninety minutes.

Extra time.

Nothing could separate them.

So once again, everything came down to penalties.

Messi stepped forward.

He took his penalty.

It did not go in.

Argentina lost again.

Three consecutive major finals.

Three heartbreaks.

2014.

2015.

2016.

For Messi, the pain had become more than football.

The player who seemed almost impossible to stop with Barcelona could not find the final piece with Argentina.

And then, something happened.

Messi said he was stepping away from international football.

The boy who had once dreamed of representing Argentina was suddenly walking away from the national team.

He had tried.

He had reached finals.

He had carried the number 10.

But the trophy still refused to come.

Maybe, for a moment, he believed that the dream was over.

But Argentina was not finished with him.

And perhaps, somewhere inside Messi, the boy from Rosario was not finished either.

He returned.

He put the shirt on again.

He wore the number 10 again.

And he walked back onto the pitch for Argentina.

The story had not ended.

The pain was still there.

The disappointment was still there.

But so was the dream.

And sometimes, the most important part of a dream is not how many times you achieve it.

It is how many times you are willing to return to it.

Messi had fallen short.

Three times.

But he came back.

Because the story of Argentina was not over yet.

And somewhere ahead, another final was waiting.

Another chance.

Another dream.

And perhaps, the greatest chapter of all was still unwritten.

Three finals. Three heartbreaks.

He wanted to walk away.

But he came back.

Because the boy from Rosario still believed.

08

THE RETURN

ARGENTINA — 2016–2019

For a moment, Lionel Messi thought the story was over.

Three finals.

Three heartbreaks.

And after the loss in the 2016 Copa América final, he walked away.

He said he was done with the Argentina national team.

The number 10 would no longer belong to the boy who had dreamed of wearing it for his country.

At least, that was how it seemed.

But some stories refuse to end when we want them to.

Sixty-six days later, Messi returned.

Argentina.

Uruguay.

The shirt was back on his shoulders.

The number 10 was back.

And the boy from Rosario was back where he had always wanted to be.

The match was difficult.

The pressure was enormous.

But then Messi received the ball.

One moment.

One touch.

One shot.

Goal.

Argentina won 1–0.

He had returned.

Not with a trophy.

Not with a final victory.

Just with a football, a shirt, and another chance.

Sometimes, another chance is enough.

But the road ahead was still difficult.

Argentina needed to qualify for the 2018 World Cup.

And as the qualifying campaign reached its final night, everything was still uncertain.

Ecuador.

Away from home.

Argentina needed something special.

And once again, Messi stepped forward.

Ecuador scored first.

Argentina were behind.

Then Messi answered.

Once.

Twice.

Three times.

A hat-trick.

Argentina won 3–1.

And the World Cup dream stayed alive.

The same player who had once walked away from Argentina was now the reason they were going to Russia.

But football was not finished testing him.

Russia.

The 2018 World Cup.

Argentina struggled through the group stage.

But they survived.

And Messi was still there.

Still wearing number 10.

Still carrying the weight of a nation.

Then came France.

The Round of 16.

A match full of goals.

A match full of hope.

But when it ended, Argentina had lost 4–3.

The World Cup was over.

Again, Messi had come close.

Again, he went home without the trophy.

Then came 2019.

Another Copa América.

Another opportunity.

Argentina reached the third-place match.

They defeated Chile 2–1.

But once again, the biggest trophy remained out of reach.

Messi had returned.

He had fought.

He had carried on.

But the dream was still waiting.

And perhaps, that was the most important part of the story.

Because returning did not mean that everything suddenly became easy.
